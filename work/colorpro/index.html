04 Case study

ColorPro: turning a product catalogue into a creator community

A professional-monitor brand's site sold specs to people who buy on identity. I led the UX of a community-first rebuild (regional partner surveys, flows, wireframes, a tiered sign-up system), and after relaunch, page views rose over 400% and the community grew past 17,000 members in its first year.

In short

  • The sub-brand makes high-end monitors for creative professionals, but its site was organised around products, not the people it wanted as customers.
  • A content audit made the repositioning case: creatives buy from brands that feel like partners. The rebuild put an active Creator Community at the centre, with products discoverable through use cases.
  • I owned the technical requirements, regional partner surveys, flows, and wireframes, then aligned creative and development through to launch, including localisation.
  • After relaunch: page views up over 400%, engagement metrics all up, and a 17,000-member community in year one, with attribution honestly shared with the marketing campaigns that ran alongside.

Anonymised: the employer’s name is withheld; the sub-brand is public and keeps its name. Everything else is as it happened.

The problem, and what was at risk

The sub-brand sells high-end monitors to creative professionals: a buyer who chooses on identity and trust at least as much as on colour-gamut numbers. The existing site was organised the way the company thought: products first. A content audit and review made the mismatch explicit: the design was too focused on product for a brand whose stated goal was to be a partner to creatives. The business goal (an active Creator Community driving engagement, events, workshops, competitions) had no home in the IA at all.

Constraints

A global brand surface, so regional needs had to be gathered and reconciled rather than assumed; I ran surveys with partners across regions to understand what each market needed. The site had to leverage a large body of existing content rather than commission everything new. And it ships in multiple languages, so every structural decision had to survive localisation.

What I decided, and what I didn’t

Community became the centre of the IA, not a page. The rejected alternative was the obvious one: refresh the catalogue’s look and bolt on a community section. Instead the rebuild made the Creator Community a first-class destination (creator profiles, testimonials from working professionals, events and competitions) with the catalogue reachable through it. The navigation shipped as three items: Products, Creator Community, Resources.

Use-case media carries discovery. Rather than asking visitors to know which monitor they need, the homepage serves persona-based media (3D printer, screen printer, photographer, videographer, digital artist) that introduces community members and routes each audience to the right product line. The design intent, recorded at the time: leverage existing content, introduce the community, and shorten the discovery journey.

Sign-up became tiered, not a mailing list. The community model needed different kinds of relationship (partners, professional creators, budding creatives), so I designed a tiered sign-up system to support multiple types of collaboration rather than a single newsletter capture. The wireframe annotations still show the IA being argued out, including a debate about two competing pathways to the partner page, resolved before anything was built.

What happened

The relaunched site shipped, with the community at its centre. Page views rose over 400%, every engagement measure moved the right way, and the community passed 17,000 members in the first year, numbers I’ll defend with their caveat attached: integrated social and digital marketing campaigns ran alongside the relaunch, so the site shares the credit rather than claiming it. That’s not a hedge; the campaigns and the rebuild were the same strategy.

The honest limit of this project: the research that shaped it was business research (partner surveys across regions), not end-user usability testing. It’s the first thing I’d add with another cycle, and the projects elsewhere on this site show what that looks like when I get it.
